TITLE OF THE STUDY
SportsHub: A Progressive Web Application for Athlete Records and Sports Activity Management System
ABSTRACT
This study designed and implemented SportsHub, a Progressive Web Application for Surigao del Norte National High School (SNNHS). The school had already begun organizing sports clubs, but every record was still kept manually, leading to disorganized data, difficulty in monitoring athlete participation, and poor coordination of events. The system has three parts: a public landing page, an athlete dashboard, and an administrator dashboard. To join a sport, applicants submit a medical certificate, a birth certificate, and a signed parent's consent form; no account is created at that stage. The Sports Coordinator verifies each document and approves, rejects, waitlists, or returns the application for further documents, after which an athlete account is created with a one-time temporary password that must be replaced on first login. The administrator manages sports programs, coaches, events, a shared calendar, attendance sessions, medical clearances, achievements, certificate requests, and downloadable reports. Athletes check in to open attendance sessions and view their schedules, attendance rate, achievements, application feedback, and coach information. The system was built with PHP 8.2, Laravel 12, MySQL, and Tailwind CSS 4, and packaged as an installable Progressive Web Application with service-worker offline caching. Evaluation using the ISO/IEC 25010 quality model with fifteen (15) SNNHS respondents yielded an overall weighted mean of 4.46, interpreted as Agree with a quality level of Very Satisfactory, with usability scoring highest at 4.56. The system is expected to improve data organization and reduce the time spent on manual recording at SNNHS.

1. INTRODUCTION
The lack of a proper system for tracking sports activities and athlete records in Surigao del Norte National High School makes it difficult to manage sports programs, monitor athlete participation, and organize events. This study aimed to design and develop a SportsHub for the school to make sports management more organized and easier to handle. At present, the school has already started forming sports clubs, but there is still no system in place to manage records properly. Because of this, information is often scattered, and it becomes hard to keep track of athletes and scheduled activities.
Sports programs are very important for students because they help improve physical fitness, teamwork, discipline, and leadership skills. In many schools, sports-related information like player lists, schedules, and event records are still handled manually using paper or simple spreadsheets. While this method works to some extent, it can lead to errors, repeated data, and even loss of important information. It also takes more time to update records and find needed data, especially when there are many participants involved. That is why many schools are now starting to use digital systems to make their processes faster and more efficient.
In the case of Surigao del Norte National High School, the absence of a proper sports management system affects how sports clubs and activities are organized. Without a centralized platform, administrators and coaches find it challenging to monitor participation, manage events, and keep records updated. This can lead to confusion, missing information, and poor planning of activities. Developing SportsHub helped solve these problems by giving the school a single system where all sports-related data can be stored and managed properly.
This study focused on developing and testing SportsHub, a web-based Progressive Web Application that handles sports records, athlete information, coach assignments, event scheduling, attendance monitoring, medical clearances, achievements, and certificates. The system was expected to make data more organized, reduce manual work, and make it easier for users to access information. It also generates CSV and PDF reports that help in monitoring sports activities and making better decisions for future programs.

This study aims to answer the following research questions:
●	What problems are currently being experienced in managing sports activities and athlete records in Surigao del Norte National High School?
●	How can a web-based SportsHub improve the management of sports activities and athlete records?
●	How effective is the proposed system in terms of usability, reliability, and performance efficiency?
●	What improvements can be suggested for the future development of the system?

      1.1 Project Context
This project was conceptualized in response to the lack of an organized system for managing sports activities and athlete records in Surigao del Norte National High School (SNNHS). Although the school has started forming sports clubs and conducting activities, most of the records are still handled manually or written in simple lists. Because of this, it is difficult for coaches and coordinators to keep track of athletes, monitor participation, and manage schedules properly.
The project is set within the school's sports management environment, where different activities such as trainings, intramurals, and club events are regularly conducted. These activities require proper planning and organized records to run smoothly. However, without a digital system, information can become unorganized or incomplete, making it harder to retrieve when needed. This situation affects how sports programs are planned and managed in the school.
This project is important because it supports the school's move toward using technology to improve its processes. By developing SportsHub, the school now has a centralized system where all sports-related data is stored and managed in one place. This makes it easier for administrators and coaches to access information, update records, and organize activities more efficiently.
In addition, the project benefits the entire school community by improving how sports programs are monitored and managed. With a more organized system, records are more accurate and easier to maintain. This system can also serve as a foundation for future improvements and possible expansion to other school systems.


1.2	Purpose and Description
The purpose of this project is to create SportsHub, a Progressive Web Application that helps Surigao del Norte National High School (SNNHS) organize and manage its sports activities by replacing manual recording with a simple digital system. In this document, the terms Sports Coordinator, administrator, and admin refer to the same user role.
The system has three parts. The public landing page is open to any student without logging in. It shows announcements that the system publishes automatically when an event is scheduled (and withdraws if the event is cancelled or postponed), and it hosts the application form. Applicants submit a medical certificate from a licensed physician, a PSA birth certificate, and a signed parent's or guardian's consent form. Incomplete applications are not processed, and no account is created at this stage.
The administrator dashboard is accessible only to the Sports Coordinator, who moves each application through Pending, Under Review, Requires Revision, Approved, or Rejected and verifies each document. Review notes and rejection reasons are visible to the student. For approved applicants, the admin creates the athlete account with a one-time temporary password that must be replaced on first login.
The athlete dashboard is the personal space of registered athletes. Athletes can view schedules, events, announcements, attendance records, achievements, and coach information; check in to open attendance sessions; request certificates; track their application; and update their profile.
Overall, the project aims to make sports management at the school more organized, secure, and efficient, and to serve as a foundation for future expansion.


1.3 General Objectives of the Study
This study aimed to design, develop, and evaluate SportsHub, a web-based Progressive Web Application that improves the management of sports activities and athlete records at Surigao del Norte National High School (SNNHS). Specifically, it aimed to:
●	To gather data and determine system requirements – through informal observation at SNNHS and a review of related literature and DepEd sports policies, identifying problems in managing athlete records, schedules, and attendance, and defining the functional and non-functional requirements.
●	To design and develop the SportsHub system – a Progressive Web Application with application review, athlete accounts, event scheduling, attendance, medical records, achievements and certificates, reports, and an administrator dashboard, built with PHP 8.2, Laravel 12, MySQL, and Tailwind CSS 4.
●	To test and evaluate the system – measuring usability, reliability, and performance efficiency using the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model through a questionnaire, direct observation, and an automated feature test suite, and recommending improvements based on the results.

      1.4 Scope and Limitations
Scope
1.	The system covers three main user interfaces: a public-facing landing page, an athlete dashboard, and a restricted administrator dashboard accessible only to the Sports Coordinator.
2.	The landing page allows any student to view announcements, browse the available sports programs, and submit a sports application form. To be eligible to join a sport, students must submit a medical certificate, a birth certificate, and a signed parent’s or guardian’s consent form as part of their application. No account is created at this stage. Announcements are published automatically by the portal when a scheduled event is created, and are withdrawn automatically when that event is cancelled or postponed.
3.	The administrator can review, approve, reject, waitlist, or return submitted applications for further documents, verify or reject each uploaded document individually, and record review notes and rejection reasons visible to the student.
4.	Approved applicants are converted into official athlete accounts with an assigned sport, a username, and a one-time temporary password that the athlete must replace on first login.
5.	Registered athletes can log in to view their schedules, events, calendar, announcements, attendance records, achievements, application status, coach information, notifications, and the members of their own sports program, and can update profile details, check in to open attendance sessions, and request achievement certificates.
6.	The administrator dashboard manages sports programs, athlete accounts, coach assignments, event schedules, attendance sessions, medical records and injury incidents, achievements, certificate requests, a unified Messages & Follow-ups module for general communication and tracked medical follow-ups, and downloadable reports.
7.	The administrator can choose the scope of a downloaded athlete list — either the whole school or a single sport program — and can export the athlete, coach, attendance, and per-athlete attendance reports in CSV or PDF format.
8.	Attendance is tracked through attendance sessions that may belong to a scheduled event or stand alone. The roster of each session is derived automatically from the athletes assigned to the session’s sport, so no manual roster selection is required. A configurable late grace period separates a Present check-in from a Late one, and cancelled sessions are excluded from attendance-rate calculations.
9.	Medical records track examination results, clearance status, uploaded medical certificates with expiry monitoring, and scheduled check-ups. Injury incidents are recorded separately per athlete with severity, treatment, rest period, return-to-play date, and medical clearance, so that a new examination never overwrites an injury history. Choosing an athlete in the incident form selects the assigned sport of that athlete automatically.
10.	The administrator and the athlete share one Messages & Follow-ups module. General messages cover reminders, instructions, and important updates, while medical follow-ups cover checkup schedules, medical clearance follow-ups, and requests to visit the school medical clinic. A medical follow-up is tracked per athlete through its schedule, its read state, the athlete’s acknowledgment, and its completion, and it may be completed or cancelled only by an authorized administrator. The athlete receives both categories in one inbox with the notification bell, reads the medical instructions together with the checkup date, time, and location, and acknowledges a follow-up when a response is required.
11.	The system is delivered as an installable Progressive Web Application with a web app manifest and a service worker that caches the application shell and falls back to a dedicated offline page when the network is unavailable.
12.	The system was evaluated using the ISO/IEC 25010 quality standard covering usability, reliability, and performance efficiency, with fifteen (15) respondents from SNNHS including the Sports Coordinator, faculty sports advisers, and student-athletes.
Limitations
1.	The system does not include financial management, student grading features, or integration with any external platforms such as a Learning Management System (LMS) or other school information systems.
2.	A dedicated native application for Android or iOS is not part of this study. As a Progressive Web Application (PWA), the system is accessible through any standard web browser on any device, including smartphones, tablets, and laptops, without requiring additional software installation.
3.	The evaluation was conducted exclusively at SNNHS, and the results may not be generalizable to schools with different operational contexts, infrastructure, or resource availability.
4.	The accuracy of athlete records depends entirely on the administrator who manually inputs data; the system has no automated data validation against external school databases.
5.	The offline capability is a caching convenience rather than a full offline mode. Read-only screens remain browsable from the cache when the connection drops, but server-backed operations — submitting an application, checking in to a session, or approving a document — still require an active internet connection. Credentials and records are never stored on the device.

3. Technical Background
This chapter presents the technologies and tools used in the development of SportsHub, a Progressive Web Application designed for Surigao del Norte National High School. The system was developed using the Laravel Framework (version 12), Tailwind CSS, MySQL Database, Figma, and Visual Studio Code (VS Code). These technologies were carefully selected based on their reliability, accessibility, and suitability for building a secure and responsive web application. Each tool plays an important role in the development process, from designing the user interface to structuring the database and deploying the final system.
Laravel Framework (version 12)
Laravel 12 is a PHP web application framework with expressive, elegant syntax. The framework provides a robust foundation for building web applications through its built-in support for routing, authentication, database querying, caching, queueing, and automated testing. For this study, Laravel 12 served as the core of the SportsHub system because it simplifies the development of secure and maintainable applications while supporting rapid application development (RAD). The framework's MVC (Model-View-Controller) architecture separated the application logic, user interface, and data handling, which helped the developers organize the code efficiently. Laravel also provided built-in features such as Eloquent ORM for database interaction, Blade templating for rendering views, and task scheduling for automated system processes.
PHP 8.2
PHP 8.2 is the server-side scripting language used by the Laravel framework and served as the primary programming language for the SportsHub system. It runs on the Apache web server and processes requests, performs business logic, and communicates with the MySQL database to perform data operations. PHP 8.2 introduced several improvements, including readonly classes, null, false, and true as stand-alone types, and disjunctive normal form (DNF) types, which helped the developers write more efficient and reliable code. In this study, PHP 8.2 was used for creating controllers, models, routes, and other server-side components essential to the system. It was chosen because of its widespread adoption, compatibility with shared hosting environments, and strong integration with the Laravel framework.

Tailwind CSS (version 4)
Tailwind CSS version 4 is a utility-first CSS framework that allows developers to build custom user interfaces directly in their markup without writing traditional CSS files. Instead of using pre-designed component classes, Tailwind provides small utility classes such as flex, pt-4, text-center, and font-bold that can be combined to create unique designs. This approach gives developers complete control over the appearance of the interface while keeping the codebase lightweight and free of unused styles. Tailwind CSS was used in this study to design and style the user interface of SportsHub, including the layout, buttons, forms, navigation bars, tables, and responsive design. Tailwind CSS supports responsive design, allowing the system to adapt seamlessly to different screen sizes such as desktops, tablets, and smartphones. This capability was essential in ensuring that athletes, coaches, and administrators could access SportsHub from any device.
MySQL Database
MySQL is an open-source relational database management system that uses Structured Query Language (SQL) for managing and retrieving data. It is widely used in web development due to its speed, reliability, and compatibility with various programming languages and frameworks, including PHP and Laravel. In this study, MySQL served as the database management system for SportsHub, storing all critical data such as user accounts, athlete records, event schedules, attendance logs, medical records, achievements, and announcements. The database was designed using a relational structure to ensure that data is organized, consistent, and easily accessible. MySQL was chosen for its stability, ease of use, and seamless integration with the Laravel framework through Eloquent ORM.
Figma
Figma is a web-based design and prototyping tool used for creating user interface designs, wireframes, and interactive prototypes. It allows designers to collaborate in real time and produces high-fidelity mockups that closely resemble the final application. In this study, Figma was used to design the user interface of SportsHub, including the landing page, athlete dashboard, and administrator dashboard. The prototypes created in Figma served as the visual guide for the development phase and were later presented to the respondents for feedback before the final system was implemented.
Visual Studio Code (VS Code)
Visual Studio Code is a lightweight yet powerful source code editor developed by Microsoft. It supports multiple programming languages and offers features such as syntax highlighting, IntelliSense, debugging, and extensions that enhance developer productivity. In this study, VS Code served as the primary integrated development environment (IDE) for writing and editing the source code of SportsHub. Its built-in terminal and Git integration also made it convenient for the researchers to manage version control and test the system during development.
Progressive Web Application (PWA)
A Progressive Web Application is a type of web application that uses modern web capabilities to deliver an experience similar to that of a native mobile application. PWAs are built with standard web technologies such as HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, but they are enhanced with features like a web app manifest, service workers, and offline caching. A web app manifest is a JSON file that defines how the application should behave when installed on a device, including its name, icons, display mode, and background color. Service workers are scripts that run in the background and enable features such as offline access, push notifications, and resource caching. In this study, SportsHub was configured to function as a Progressive Web Application so that users could install it on their devices and access it through a browser without requiring a download from an application store. This approach was chosen because it allows the system to reach users across different devices and platforms while reducing development and maintenance costs. Although the application shell is cached and remains browsable when the connection drops, the offline capability is a caching convenience rather than a full offline mode. Server-backed operations such as submitting an application, checking in to an attendance session, or approving a document still require an active internet connection, and credentials and records are never stored on the device.
Apache Web Server
Apache is an open-source web server software that delivers web content to users over the internet or local network. It is one of the most widely used web servers in the world and is commonly paired with PHP and MySQL in web development environments. In this study, Apache served as the web server that hosted the SportsHub application. It processed incoming HTTP requests from users, executed the PHP scripts, and returned the appropriate web pages to the browser. Apache was used through the XAMPP local server environment during development and testing.
XAMPP Local Server
XAMPP is a free and open-source cross-platform web server solution stack package that bundles Apache, MySQL, PHP, and Perl. It allows developers to create and test web applications locally without the need for an internet connection or a remote server. In this study, XAMPP served as the local development environment where the SportsHub application was built and tested before deployment. The researchers used XAMPP to run the Apache web server, manage the MySQL database, and execute PHP scripts during the development process.


3.1 Conceptual Framework 
The Input-Process-Output-Evaluation (IPO-E) model was used as the conceptual framework of the study. The IPO-E model is a systems approach that describes how a system transforms inputs into outputs through a series of processes, which are then evaluated to determine the quality and effectiveness of the system. This model provides a clear and structured way of understanding how the proposed system works and how it can be assessed.
The Input component of the framework refers to the resources, data, and requirements that are fed into the system. These include the sports activity records, athlete profiles, coach assignments, event schedules, school policies, user requirements, and the technological tools used in the development of the system. The inputs also include the data gathered from the respondents through observation and informal consultation, which helped identify the features and functions that the system should provide.
The Process component describes the series of activities and operations performed on the inputs to produce the desired outputs. In this study, the process involved the analysis of requirements, design of the user interface using Figma, development of the system using the Laravel framework, database design using MySQL, coding of the application logic using PHP 8.2, styling of the interface using Tailwind CSS 4, testing of the system, and deployment as a Progressive Web Application. These processes were carried out following the Rapid Application Development (RAD) methodology, which allowed the researchers to develop the system incrementally and incorporate feedback at each stage.
The Output component refers to the results produced by the system after the processes have been applied. The outputs of this study include the fully developed SportsHub application with its three main interfaces: the public landing page, the athlete dashboard, and the administrator dashboard. The system produces organized and centralized records of athletes, sports programs, coaches, events, attendance, medical records, achievements, and certificates. It also produces downloadable reports in CSV and PDF formats that can be used by the school for monitoring and decision-making purposes.
The Evaluation component assesses the quality and effectiveness of the outputs based on established criteria. In this study, the system was evaluated using the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model, which measures the software in terms of usability, reliability, and performance efficiency. The evaluation results were used to determine whether the system met the requirements and expectations of the respondents, and to identify areas that may be improved in future versions of the system. 4. METHODOLOGY
This chapter presents the research method, development model, research locale, respondents, data gathering instrument, statistical tools, and evaluation procedures used in the development and evaluation of SportsHub. The study followed the Rapid Application Development (RAD) model, which enabled the researchers to design, develop, test, and finalize the system within a compressed timeframe through iterative feedback and close collaboration with the end users.
Research Method
This study used the developmental research method, specifically the research and development (R&D) approach. Developmental research is commonly used in studies that aim to create a product, system, or innovation and evaluate its effectiveness. In this study, the researchers planned, designed, developed, tested, and evaluated SportsHub. The system was developed based on the actual needs of Surigao del Norte National High School in managing its sports activities and athlete records.
Research and Development Model
The Rapid Application Development (RAD) model was used as the framework for the development of SportsHub. RAD is a software development methodology that emphasizes rapid prototyping and quick feedback over long planning and strict development cycles. It consists of four phases: Requirements Planning, Rapid Prototyping (Design and Development), Feedback (User Testing), and Finalization (Implementation). This model was chosen because it allows the researchers to build working prototypes early, present them to the users, gather feedback, and make improvements before the final system is implemented. 4.1 Planning Requirements
The planning phase will focus on identifying the functional and non-functional requirements of the SportsHub. Instead of relying solely on formal interviews or surveys, the requirements for this project are largely based on the personal experience of the researchers as former student-athletes in a Philippine public high school setting, particularly in schools similar to SNNHS in terms of context and available resources.
As former student-athletes, the researchers have personally experienced the challenges of manually managed sports programs. Records of sports participation were often written on paper or encoded in basic spreadsheets, making it difficult to find information when needed. Coach assignments were communicated informally, and event schedules were posted on bulletin boards or shared through word-of-mouth. Athletes frequently missed or forgot upcoming events because there was no centralized notification or scheduling system in place. These real-life experiences provided a grounded and realistic basis for defining what the system needs to accomplish.
To support this experiential foundation, the researchers also conducted informal observation of the current sports management practices at SNNHS. Through these observations, it was confirmed that the school had already started organizing sports clubs but continued to rely on manual documentation. Coaches and coordinators were found to have difficulty consolidating records, tracking which athletes were active, and keeping event information up to date. These observations helped validate the requirements identified through the researchers’ personal experience and gave additional context specific to SNNHS.
Based on these inputs, the following key requirements were identified for the SportsHub:
Functional Requirements
Admin / Sports Coordinator:
●	Secure login and authentication for the Administrator or Sports Coordinator, with a self-service password reset link and an automatic warning when a wrong password is entered
●	View and manage student applications submitted from the landing page, moving each one through Pending, Under Review, Requires Revision, Approved, or Rejected
●	Verify or reject each uploaded eligibility document individually, request missing documents, and record review notes and rejection reasons that the student can read
●	Create official athlete accounts for approved applicants (name, email, student ID, assigned sport, username, and a one-time temporary password)
●	Reset an athlete’s password from the Athlete Management module and require the athlete to set a new one on first login
●	Manage sports programs (Add, Edit, Delete, View) including classification, gender grouping, status, and logo
●	Manage athlete profiles and account details, including contact number, emergency contact, and profile photo
●	Assign coaches to specific sports, distinguishing Head Coaches from Assistant Coaches, and manage their profiles and status
●	Schedule and manage sports events such as trainings, tournaments, intramurals, tryouts, and meetings

Security implementation. Athlete access is enforced at the query level through model scopes that always receive the authenticated account rather than an identifier supplied by the browser. 4.5 Evaluation Method and Tools
The quality of the SportsHub was evaluated using the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model, an internationally recognized standard for software product quality. Three characteristics were selected for their relevance to the system's purpose and users: Usability (can the coordinator, advisers, and athletes operate the system without technical training), Reliability (does it perform consistently without data loss or errors), and Performance Efficiency (does it respond within acceptable time under a standard connection).
The primary tool was a fifteen-item questionnaire (five items per characteristic) on a five-point Likert scale from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree), developed by the research team and validated by the research adviser. Fifteen (15) SNNHS respondents answered it after a brief orientation and hands-on use of all major modules: the designated Sports Coordinator, three faculty sports advisers, and eleven student-athletes from different sports. The team also directly observed the testing session, noting ease of navigation, technical errors, and responsiveness on the school network, and used these notes to give context to the questionnaire results.
Responses were analyzed using the weighted mean, computed as the sum of each rating multiplied by its frequency divided by the number of respondents (Σfw / N), for each item, each characteristic, and all items combined. Means were interpreted using Table 2. The results determined whether the SportsHub met the quality standards required for practical deployment in a school setting.

The key relationships are as follows. A Sport has many athletes (Users), applications, coaches, events, attendance sessions, and achievements, and many announcements through its events. An athlete has many attendance records, medical records, medical incidents, applications, and achievements. A Coach belongs to a Sport and has many events. An Event belongs to a Sport and a Coach and has many attendance sessions, attendance records, and achievements. An Achievement belongs to an athlete (or names a team), a Sport, and an Event, and has many certificate requests. Medical records and medical incidents each belong to an athlete, so a new examination never overwrites an injury history. A Message belongs to the sender and has many message recipients, one for each athlete it reached, and it may have one medical follow-up, which in turn has many follow-up recipients recording the read state, the acknowledgment, and the completion of every athlete; a reply is stored as a message that points back to the message it answers. All relationships are implemented as foreign key constraints in MySQL and Eloquent associations in Laravel.

c.) Sequence Diagram	
The Sequence Diagram (Figures 12 to 14) documents the message flow between actors, Laravel request handlers, services, and the database for three of the system's most frequent and sensitive interactions.
●       Athlete check-in. The athlete opens the Attendance Record page and submits a check-in. The attendance session service locks the session, verifies that it is open, not cancelled, and dated today, and that the athlete's sport matches the session's sport. It then saves the record as Present within the late grace period or Late afterwards. Duplicate check-ins are rejected with a clear message, and the Sports Coordinator is notified.
●       Application approval and account creation. The coordinator reviews the three documents and approves the application. The controller records the decision, notes, and reviewer, then creates the athlete account with the assigned sport, a generated username, a one-time temporary password, and the must_change_password flag. The athlete is notified in-app, and on first login middleware redirects to the change-password screen before any other page.
●       Certificate request and issuance. The athlete requests a certificate for an achievement, creating a Pending request and notifying the coordinators. The coordinator approves it, the certificate service assembles the payload (recipient, achievement, reference number, signatories, issue date), and the PDF is rendered and stored on private storage. Releasing and issuing it marks the request Issued and makes the file downloadable on the athlete's Achievements page.

5.5 The SportsHub Progressive Web Application
SportsHub was delivered as an installable Progressive Web Application instead of a native Android or iOS app. Students, coaches, and the Sports Coordinator can therefore use it on any device through a standard browser, without an app-store download and without the cost of maintaining separate native codebases. It can be installed from the browser to the home screen of a phone, tablet, or laptop and opens in a standalone window.
Two standard components provide this behavior. The web app manifest defines the application name, home-screen short name, display mode, colors, orientation, and icons so the installed app shows the identity of SNNHS. The service worker runs in the background and intercepts network requests: it uses a cache-first strategy for the static shell (stylesheets, scripts, icons), so the interface appears immediately, and a network-first strategy for dynamic pages and data. When the connection is unavailable, it shows a dedicated offline page that explains that the connection was lost and the content could not be loaded.
Server-backed operations such as submitting an application, checking in to a session, or approving a document still require an internet connection, and credentials and records are never stored on the device. The offline page is deliberately a simple notice rather than a simulated interface, because an interactive screen that cannot submit data would mislead the athlete.
The approach also simplifies deployment: because the system is a single web application on a central server, an update by the developers reaches every user the next time the app is opened, without asking students to install a new version. This is a significant advantage for a school, where coordinating updates across many personal devices would be impractical.
6. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION
6.1 Conclusion
Based on the development and evaluation of the SportsHub, the researchers concluded the following:
First, the system provides SNNHS with a centralized platform that addresses the problems identified in the study: scattered manual athlete records, difficulty monitoring attendance, informal handling of event schedules, and untracked medical clearances. Sports data can now be stored, managed, and retrieved in one place. The improvement in workload and data accuracy was assessed through user perception; it was not measured against a before-and-after baseline.
Second, the system was developed successfully using PHP 8.2, Laravel 12, Tailwind CSS 4, and MySQL. The public landing page, athlete dashboard, and administrator dashboard were implemented as designed and tested, and the system is delivered as an installable Progressive Web Application accessible on any device through a standard browser.
Third, the features identified during requirements planning were implemented and tested: sports program management, athlete records, coach assignments, event scheduling, a shared calendar, attendance monitoring, medical records, achievements, certificates, announcements, and downloadable CSV and PDF reports.
Fourth, the evaluation rated the system favorably, with an overall weighted mean of 4.46 (Agree / Very Satisfactory). Usability ranked highest at 4.56 (Excellent), followed by reliability (4.45) and performance efficiency (4.37). The lowest-rated items, recovery from interruptions (4.20) and handling of large data volumes (4.13), identify where future improvement is needed.
6.2 Recommendation
Based on the conclusions, the researchers recommend the following:
First, the school should adopt the SportsHub for its sports management and designate a person, such as the Sports Coordinator, to manage and maintain it so that the data stay accurate and up to date. The school should also provide the necessary infrastructure, such as computers and a stable internet connection.
Second, the developers should implement the improvements identified in the evaluation: a clearer in-app message when a connection is lost, optimized report and athlete-list generation through pagination and indexed queries, and a first-login guide for the mandatory password change.
Third, the system may be expanded with email or SMS notifications, a mobile application version, integration with other school systems, a financial module for registration fees and equipment inventory, and an analytics dashboard for tracking athlete performance over time.
Fourth, the system should be tested in other schools with different operational contexts and with a larger group of respondents to determine its generalizability. A longitudinal study comparing sports management before and after adoption is also recommended to measure its long-term effects.
Finally, future researchers who wish to develop a similar system may consider the technologies and RAD methodology used in this study, which proved suitable for building a secure, reliable, and maintainable web-based application.
